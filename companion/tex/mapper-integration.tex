\section{Boundary Mapper Integration}

The Boundary Mapper is a diagnostic instrument that reads geometric,
spectral, holonomic, and semantic invariants across the A6 manifold. It does
not enforce structural constraints; instead, it relies on the A6 firewall to
ensure that the manifold remains stable enough to be diagnostically readable.

\subsection{Diagnostic vs. Structural Roles}

The A6 firewall is \textbf{structural}. It enforces continuity, stability,
and invariant preservation. The Boundary Mapper is \textbf{diagnostic}. It
measures curvature, holonomy, drift, collapse locality, and invariant lift.

\begin{itemize}
    \item The firewall \emph{prevents} collapse.
    \item The Mapper \emph{detects} collapse.
    \item The firewall \emph{preserves} invariants.
    \item The Mapper \emph{reads} invariants.
    \item The firewall \emph{regulates} transitions.
    \item The Mapper \emph{classifies} transitions.
\end{itemize}

This separation ensures that diagnostic operations do not induce geometric
fracture.

\subsection{Firewall Dependencies}

The Boundary Mapper depends on the firewall through five primary invariants:

\paragraph{Curvature Continuity}
The Mapper assumes curvature varies continuously:
\[
K(p) \approx K(q).
\]
This allows the Mapper to detect curvature anomalies without inducing
geometric tearing.

\paragraph{Spectral Continuity}
The Mapper assumes operator spectra remain continuous:
\[
\mathrm{Spec}(L(p)) \text{ continuous in } p.
\]
This enables spectral mismatch diagnostics.

\paragraph{Holonomy Bound}
The Mapper assumes holonomy remains below fracture thresholds:
\[
H(\gamma) < \Theta_{\max}.
\]
This ensures drift accumulation does not destabilize diagnostic loops.

\paragraph{Collapse Locality}
The Mapper assumes collapse surfaces are locally detectable:
\[
p \in \Sigma \Rightarrow B_\epsilon(p) \cap \Sigma \neq \emptyset.
\]
This allows the Mapper to map collapse surfaces without smearing.

\paragraph{Invariant Lift}
The Mapper assumes modal, semantic, and provenance invariants lift correctly:
\[
I_{\mathrm{sem}}(M) \Rightarrow I_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathcal{A}(M,S)).
\]
This ensures semantic diagnostics remain meaningful.

\subsection{Diagnostic Vector Alignment}

The Boundary Mapper computes a diagnostic vector:
\[
D(x) = (\kappa, \Delta H, \lambda, \varepsilon_{fp}, \varepsilon_{inv}),
\]
where:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\kappa$ measures curvature deviation,
    \item $\Delta H$ measures holonomy drift,
    \item $\lambda$ measures collapse-surface proximity,
    \item $\varepsilon_{fp}$ measures fixed-point violation,
    \item $\varepsilon_{inv}$ measures irreversibility.
\end{itemize}

Each component of $D(x)$ assumes firewall invariants hold. Without the
firewall, $D(x)$ would be undefined or unstable.

\subsection{Why the Mapper Must Not Become the Firewall}

The Boundary Mapper is intentionally non-structural:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Readability:} Diagnostic tools must remain interpretable.
    \item \textbf{Modularity:} Structural and diagnostic layers must remain
          separable.
    \item \textbf{Safety:} Diagnostics must not modify manifold geometry.
    \item \textbf{Stability:} Structural enforcement must remain independent
          of diagnostic measurement.
\end{enumerate}

If the Mapper attempted to enforce firewall constraints, it would become
non-diagnostic and risk inducing collapse.

\section{Integration Summary}

The Boundary Mapper relies on the A6 firewall to maintain geometric,
spectral, holonomic, and semantic stability. The firewall ensures that the
manifold remains readable; the Mapper provides diagnostic classification of
curvature, drift, collapse surfaces, and invariant lift. This separation of
roles ensures both structural safety and diagnostic clarity.
